Practical Solutions

Practical, evidence-based ways to evaluate biotechnology information, discuss risks and benefits and communicate responsibly.

TAKE ACTION

How to engage with biotechnology responsibly.

Good science communication is not about saying “yes” or “no” to everything. It is about asking better questions.

01

Check the source

Prefer universities, government agencies, scientific organisations and peer-reviewed literature for scientific claims.

02

Separate fact from opinion

Look for data, methods, uncertainty and whether a claim is actually supported by the cited evidence.

03

Consider the context

A technology can have benefits and risks at the same time. Ask who benefits, what could go wrong and how risks are managed.

04

Don't spread sensational claims

Headlines can simplify complex research. Read beyond the headline before sharing biotechnology content.

05

Respect ethical questions

Questions about animals, human applications, access and environmental effects are legitimate parts of responsible biotechnology discussion.

06

Keep learning

Science changes. Revisit credible sources when new evidence, regulations or technologies emerge.

A simple checklist

  1. What exactly is the technology?
  2. What biological mechanism is involved?
  3. What evidence supports the claimed benefit?
  4. What risks or uncertainties remain?
  5. Who regulates or monitors it?
