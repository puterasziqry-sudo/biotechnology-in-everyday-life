Cloning & Gene Editing

A detailed look at Dolly the Sheep, somatic cell nuclear transfer, cloning, CRISPR and the difference between cloning and gene editing.

Dolly was born at the Roslin Institute on 5 July 1996 and became the first mammal cloned from an adult cell.
THE DOLLY STORY

Why Dolly changed biology.

Dolly was created using somatic cell nuclear transfer (SCNT). A nucleus from an adult mammary-gland cell was transferred into an egg cell whose nucleus had been removed.

The experiment showed that the nucleus of a specialised adult cell could be reprogrammed to support development of a whole animal. Roslin describes Dolly as the first mammal cloned from an adult cell.

Key distinction: cloning copies genetic information from a donor nucleus; genetic modification changes DNA to introduce or alter a trait. They are not the same thing.
SCNT STEP-BY-STEP

How Dolly was made

Based on explanations from the Roslin Institute and National Museums Scotland.

01

Adult donor cell

A cell was taken from the mammary gland of a six-year-old Finn Dorset sheep.

02

Egg cell prepared

An egg cell from a Scottish Blackface sheep had its own nucleus removed.

03

Nucleus transferred

The donor nucleus was introduced into the enucleated egg.

04

Electrical stimulation

An electrical pulse helped fuse and activate the reconstructed cell so it could begin dividing.

05

Embryo development

After development was confirmed in the laboratory, the embryo was prepared for transfer.

06

Surrogate pregnancy

The embryo was transferred to a surrogate Scottish Blackface ewe, and Dolly was born on 5 July 1996.

Dolly's parents in the process

The nuclear DNA donor was a Finn Dorset sheep. The egg came from a Scottish Blackface sheep, and a Scottish Blackface ewe carried the pregnancy.

Was Dolly exactly identical?

Almost all of a cell's DNA is nuclear. A small amount is mitochondrial DNA from the egg cell, so a clone is not literally identical in every molecule.

What happened later?

Dolly lived until 14 February 2003 and produced six lambs. Her story continues to influence discussions about cloning, reprogramming and ethics.

Cloning vs CRISPR

Cloning: aims to reproduce an organism using genetic material from a donor, as in SCNT.

Gene editing: changes a selected DNA sequence. CRISPR-Cas systems can be used as molecular tools for targeted genome editing.
