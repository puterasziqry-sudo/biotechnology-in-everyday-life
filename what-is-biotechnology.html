Introduction

What Is Biotechnology?

A clear introduction to biotechnology, its biological foundations, major applications and why the field matters.

THE BASICS

What does biotechnology mean?

Biotechnology is technology based on biology. It uses cellular and molecular processes to develop products, services and solutions.

People have used biological processes for centuries — for example, fermentation in bread, yoghurt and other foods. Modern biotechnology extends these ideas using molecular biology, genetics, cell culture and advanced analytical tools.

Modern biotechnology combines biology with laboratory and analytical technologies.
KEY CONCEPTS

Four ideas to remember

DNA

Genetic information

DNA stores biological instructions that influence how cells develop and function.

CELL

Cells

Cells are living systems that can be grown, modified or used to produce useful biological materials.

BIO

Biological processes

Microbes, enzymes and cells can perform reactions that are useful in food, medicine and industry.

TECH

Technology

Modern tools make it possible to measure, edit, culture and analyse biological systems more precisely.

Why biotechnology matters

Biotechnology can contribute to disease prevention and treatment, crop improvement, food production, environmental management and scientific research. Its benefits and risks depend on the specific application, how it is developed and how it is regulated.

Think scientifically: avoid judging all biotechnology as one single category. A vaccine, a fermented food, a GM crop and a cloned animal are different applications with different questions to evaluate.
